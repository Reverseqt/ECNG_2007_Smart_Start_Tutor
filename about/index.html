About & credits

The SmartStart Digital Electronics Tutor was created as a Flash-based learning aid for digital electronics students. This edition rebuilds it for modern browsers so it can go on being used.

Credits

Neela Seegobin — original author. The tutor's structure, worked examples, exercises and answer logic are hers, and the reconstruction follows them closely.

Azim Abdool — web edition. Translation of the original Flash source into HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SVG, and the interface presented here.

Published by the IEEE EDS Trinidad and Tobago Chapter. Chapter branding is applied to the interface only; IEEE did not author the original tutor.

How the rebuild was done

The original was distributed as a Shockwave application. Its instructional text and ActionScript were recovered from the source .fla file and reimplemented as ordinary web pages, so that the material now works on phones and tablets, requires no plugin, and can be read by a screen reader.

  • The original topics, worked examples and exercise logic are retained wherever practical.
  • Flash controls are replaced with accessible, keyboard-operable web controls.
  • Artwork is redrawn in SVG only where the original vector timeline could not be recovered.
  • Broken external dependencies, including the EVITA executable launcher, were dropped rather than reproduced.
  • Electrical values are those used by the original; current data sheets should be consulted for real devices.

The activities themselves carry no commentary on any of this. They are written for students working through the subject, and the record of what was preserved is kept here instead.

What was preserved

The following material derives directly from the original tutor, and its instructional content and answer logic are reproduced as recovered.

Digital Foundations
The analogue-to-digital threshold example; the table of physical two-state technologies; the integrated-circuit classification controls for transistor technology, package and scale of integration; and representative TTL and CMOS threshold values.
Logic Fundamentals
The three-gate introduction, the list of common gate symbols, the Boolean expressions attached to each, and the De Morgan relationships.
Digital Hardware Practice
The MOSFET and CMOS explanations, the diode review, the TTL/CMOS comparison categories, floating-input behaviour and remedies, the open-collector truth table, the tri-state OR gate, the decoupling and breadboard sequence, Schmitt-trigger hysteresis, the free-wheeling diode and the LED series-resistor calculation.
Boolean Design
The notation and worked compound expression, the duality treatment, the progression from minterms and sums of products to maxterms and products of sums, the Karnaugh-map prime-implicant set, the hazard treatment and the Petrick coverage example.
Combinational Building Blocks
The 3-to-8 decoder exercise and its answer logic, the decoder-as-minterm and cascading examples, the 74×151 multiplexer activity, the demultiplexer routing logic and the multiplexer synthesis K-map.
Data Representation
The number-system demonstrations and base-conversion examples, the fixed and floating-point treatment, IEEE 754 formats, the packed and unpacked BCD distinction, the reflected Gray-code method with its rotary encoder, and the ASCII treatment.
Arithmetic and Comparison
The BCD-to-seven-segment decoder and its segment mapping, and the magnitude comparator exercise.
Sequential Logic
The random bistable power-up behaviour, the SR and D latch progression, the falling-edge D flip-flop, JK transition behaviour, the register and modulo-four counter, the Moore and Mealy definitions, the state-table derivation and the finite-string recogniser.

What was added

Some material in this edition has no counterpart in the original. It was written to close gaps that became apparent once the sequence was assembled, and follows the same conventions as the rest.

  • Signal flow through a multi-gate circuit, bridging single gates to Boolean design.
  • Exclusive-OR and exclusive-NOR, which the original did not treat.
  • Synthesis with gates, stated explicitly as the conclusion of Boolean design.
  • Signed number representations: sign-magnitude, two’s complement and biased forms.
  • Half and full adders, ripple-carry addition, subtraction by complement, the derivation of a comparator from a subtractor, and an arithmetic and logic unit.
  • Synthesis with lookup tables, and the comparison of the four synthesis methods.
  • The hexadecimal extension of the seven-segment decoder, and DIP pin numbering.

Textbooks and class notes cited by the original are collected on the references page.
