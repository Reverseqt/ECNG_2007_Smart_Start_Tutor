Part 1

From a specification to a truth table

Synthesis converts a statement of required behaviour into a realisable circuit.

Boolean algebra, canonical forms and Karnaugh maps are the instruments of synthesis: the conversion of a specification into a circuit that can be constructed. This activity carries a single function through that process from beginning to end.

Consider a three-input function F(A, B, C) required to be 1 for minterms 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7, and 0 otherwise. In the notation established earlier, F = Σm(1, 3, 5, 6, 7).

Reading off every row where F is 1 gives the canonical sum of products:

F = A′B′C + A′BC + AB′C + ABC′ + ABC

The expression is correct but uneconomical: five product terms and fifteen literals. The remainder of this activity reduces it.

Groupings

Pick a grouping to see what it contributes.

Two groups cover every 1-cell. The four-cell group loses both A and B and leaves just C. The two-cell group loses C and leaves A·B. So:

F = C + A·B

Five product terms reduce to two and fifteen literals to three. The two expressions have identical truth tables, as Part 3 confirms.

Part 4

Synthesise one yourself

A second function, following the same procedure.

Now take G(A, B, C) = Σm(0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6). Fill in the truth table, then choose the minimised expression.

G = Σm(0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6)
MintermABCG
Which minimised expression matches G?
Hint

Fill the G column first. Then look at which rows are 0: only minterms 5 and 7, which are the rows where A = 1 and C = 1. A function that is 0 exactly when A and C are both 1 is the complement of A·C, and De Morgan turns (A·C)′ into A′ + C′.

Fill in all eight rows and choose an expression, then check your work.
