Karnaugh Maps

Map truth-table outputs into Gray-code order, examine wrap-around adjacency, and identify the six prime implicants of the worked function.

Home / Boolean Design / Karnaugh Maps

Part 1

A truth table arranged for adjacency

A K-map is a graphical form of a truth table. Rows and columns use Gray-code order so adjacent cells differ in only one variable.

Explore adjacency

Select any cell. Its horizontal and vertical neighbours will be highlighted—including neighbours across the outer edges.

Select a cell to begin.

Adjacent cellDiagonal cells are not adjacent

Why the edges connect: the map wraps horizontally and vertically. The left and right edges meet, and the top and bottom edges meet.

Part 2

Find the original six prime implicants

The function is F = Σm(0,1,2,4,7,8,9,11,13,14,15). Select all cells in one prime implicant, then check the group.

Find any largest rectangular group containing 1-cells only. Groups may wrap around the edges.

Prime implicants identified

A group of 2k cells eliminates k changing variables from its product term. Prime implicants may overlap, but one should not be completely contained in another.

Part 3

Using don't-care conditions

A don't-care cell may be treated as 1 where doing so forms a larger implicant, but it need not be covered.

While finding prime implicants

Treat a don't-care as either 0 or 1—whichever produces a simpler result. Do not select a group containing only don't-care cells.

While selecting essential prime implicants

Treat don't-cares as 0. A don't-care covered by only one prime implicant is not a distinguished 1-cell.

Completion check
Find the prime implicants and answer both questions.
