Part 1

The bit as a unit

A bit holds one of two states. All larger quantities are formed by grouping bits.

One bit can be in one of two states, written 0 and 1. Put two bits side by side and there are four possible patterns; three bits give eight. Each bit you add doubles the number of distinct patterns the group can take.

3 bits

These patterns are just the distinct arrangements of the bits. What a pattern means — a number, a character, a colour — is a separate decision, covered in Data Representation.
