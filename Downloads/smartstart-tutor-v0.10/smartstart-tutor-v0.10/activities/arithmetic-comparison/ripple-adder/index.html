Four-Bit Ripple-Carry Addition

Connecting each carry output to the next carry input extends a single-column adder to four bits. The carry propagates across the row as it does in manual addition.

Home / Arithmetic and Comparison / Four-Bit Ripple-Carry Addition

Part 1

Chaining full adders

One full adder handles a single column; four handle four bits.

Manual addition proceeds column by column from the right, carrying into the next column as required. A four-bit adder performs the same operation in hardware: four full adders in parallel, with each carry output connected to the carry input of the column to its left.

Part 2

When the answer does not fit

Four bits represent 0 to 15; not every sum lies within that range.

If the carry out of the most significant column is 1, the correct result required a fifth bit. The four sum bits then hold the true value less 16, which is incorrect if a result within range was expected.

For this reason a practical adder brings the final carry out to a pin rather than discarding it. In unsigned arithmetic that output is the overflow indication, and software is expected to test it.

Propagation delay

No column can settle until the carry from the column to its right has arrived. The carry therefore propagates across the width of the adder one stage at a time, and a wide ripple-carry adder is correspondingly slow. Faster arrangements, such as carry-lookahead, compute the carries in parallel at the cost of considerably more logic.
