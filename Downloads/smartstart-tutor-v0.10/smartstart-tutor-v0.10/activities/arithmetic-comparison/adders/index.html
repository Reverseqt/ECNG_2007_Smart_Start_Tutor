Half and Full Adders

Addition requires the circuit to interpret its inputs as numbers. Two gates form a half adder, and two half adders form the full adder from which wider adders are built.

Part 1

The half adder

The sum of two bits requires two output bits, since 1 + 1 cannot be expressed in one.

The addition of two single bits admits four cases: 0+0=0, 0+1=1, 1+0=1 and 1+1=10 in binary. The final case requires two output bits, conventionally termed the sum and the carry.

The sum column across the four cases reads 0, 1, 1, 0, which is 1 exactly when the inputs differ: this is the XOR function. The carry column reads 0, 0, 0, 1, which is 1 only when both inputs are 1: this is the AND function. The complete circuit therefore comprises two gates.

It is termed a half adder because it cannot accept a carry from a preceding column, which restricts its use to the least significant bit position.

Part 2

The full adder

Add a third input so a column can accept the carry from the column to its right.

In the manual addition of multi-digit numbers each column receives three quantities: the two digits and any carry from the column to its right. A full adder reproduces this, and therefore has three inputs: A, B and Cin.

A full adder may be constructed from two half adders. The first forms the sum of A and B; the second adds that result to Cin. A carry from either half adder constitutes a carry out of the column, so the two carries are combined by an OR gate.
