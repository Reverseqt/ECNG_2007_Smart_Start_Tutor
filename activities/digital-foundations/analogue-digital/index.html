Part 1

Analogue and digital values

Analogue values vary continuously. Digital values are discrete: only particular states or levels are recognised.

The difference is most clearly seen by mapping a range of analogue voltages onto logic 0 and logic 1. Drag the slider and watch which logic level the voltage is read as.

Where the thresholds sit is not a law of nature — it is a property of the logic family the chip belongs to. Change the family below and watch the bands move.

Part 2

Check your understanding

The same voltage does not mean the same thing to every family.

Which description best matches a digital value?
A signal sitting at 1.5 V is applied to an input of each family below. How is it read?
  • TTL, 5 V supply (LOW ≤ 0.8 V, HIGH ≥ 2.0 V)
  • CMOS, 5 V supply (LOW ≤ 1.5 V, HIGH ≥ 3.5 V)
  • LVCMOS, 1.8 V supply (LOW ≤ 0.63 V, HIGH ≥ 1.17 V)
Hint

Set the slider to 1.5 V in Part 1 and switch families without moving it. One voltage, three different answers. A voltage between the two thresholds is not a third logic value — it is a voltage the input is not guaranteed to read correctly either way.
